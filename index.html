The Ghosts of Chinatown

A semi-staged song cycle for bass-baritone, viola, and piano by Daixuan Ai, on the 1871 Los Angeles Chinese Massacre. A photographer develops his last pictures of a building marked for demolition, and the eighteen people killed on that ground speak through him.

Through 2029, The Ghosts of Chinatown is performed exclusively by Dashon Burton, David Samuel, and Sun-A Park. Presenting the work means engaging this trio, with the composer available to join for talks and residency activity.

01 The work

Eighteen names, one darkroom

On the night of October 24, 1871, a mob of about five hundred Angelenos descended on Calle de los Negros and killed eighteen Chinese immigrants. It is one of the largest mass lynchings in American history, and one of the least remembered.

The cycle follows three of the dead: Dr. Gene Tong, an herbalist who treated Chinese, white, and Hispanic patients alike; Ah Wing, a worker at the Pico House hotel and the first to be lynched; and an unnamed tong fighter who believes he deserves to be forgotten. The photographer allows each spirit to possess him, so a single singer carries every voice.

The refrain gives the finale its title: to forgive is to begin to remember. The closing funeral procession quotes the Chinese dirge Āiyuè, offering the victims the farewell they were denied.

Composer

Daixuan Ai

Chinese-American composer whose work addresses identity, violence, and erased histories. Recipient of the 2025 BMI Foundation Ellen Taaffe Zwilich Award for Silent Theater. Performed by the Chicago Symphony Civic Orchestra, Indianapolis Symphony, and Albany Symphony. Doctoral candidate at Indiana University Jacobs School of Music.

Website
Librettist

Dennis Yueh-Yeh Li

Director and playwright, originally from Taiwan, whose work explores diaspora, power, and immersive storytelling. Affiliated with the Museum of Chinese in America, he conceived the libretto for The Ghosts of Chinatown—the photographer whose images catch the wandering ghosts of the massacre. MA in Performance Studies, New York University.

03 Technical overview

What the stage needs

The staging is spare by design: two playing zones, a work table for the photographer's darkroom, and an isolated chair downstage where each possession takes place. A full stage plan and tech rider are available on request.

  • InstrumentConcert grand piano with open lid access; inside-the-piano techniques are used
  • ProjectionProjector and upstage screen for archival photographs that develop and fade on cue
  • LightingRed darkroom wash, one spotlight, and a strobe effect in movement 4
  • PropsWork table, chair, developing trays, and a clothesline for hanging prints
  • SpaceRecital halls, black boxes, museum auditoriums, and chamber series venues
  • AdvisoryDepicts racial violence and lynching; includes strobe lighting. Recommended for ages 14 and up.
