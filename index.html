The Ghosts of Chinatown

A semi-staged song cycle for bass-baritone, viola, and piano on the 1871 Los Angeles Chinese Massacre. Daixuan Ai’s Ghost of Chinatown is vocal chamber music conceived as a one-man opera, weaving history, theater, projected imagery, and virtuosic writing into a single unbroken act.

In his darkroom, a photographer develops his last pictures of a neighborhood marked for demolition, and the dead of that ground begin to speak through him...

02 The work

Eighteen names, one darkroom

On the night of October 24, 1871, a mob of about five hundred Angelenos descended on Calle de los Negros and killed eighteen Chinese immigrants. It is one of the largest mass lynchings in American history, and one of the least remembered.

The cycle follows three of the dead: Dr. Gene Tong, an herbalist who treated Chinese, white, and Hispanic patients alike; Ah Wing, a worker at the Pico House hotel and the first to be lynched; and an unnamed tong fighter who believes he deserves to be forgotten. The photographer allows each spirit to possess him, so a single singer carries every voice.

The refrain gives the finale its title: to forgive is to begin to remember. The closing funeral procession quotes the Chinese dirge Āiyuè, offering the victims the farewell they were denied.

03 Why now

Los Angeles is remembering, and the world arrives in 2028

For 150 years, one bronze plaque marked the massacre. Now the city is building its first full memorial, a bestselling novelist has brought the story to a national readership, and LA28 will put Los Angeles history in front of a global audience. The 2027–28 and 2028–29 seasons sit right in that window.

The scale 1 in 10
Killed, 18Chinese Angelenos, c. 200

In one night the mob killed 18 of about 200 Chinese residents of Los Angeles. The mob itself, about 500 people, was close to a tenth of the whole town.

The memorial 1 → 18

The planned memorial places 18 granite banyan trunks on the former Calle de los Negros, one per victim, with satellite sites from El Pueblo to Union Station. The 1871 Memorial Project is raising $12 million to finish it before the Games.

The book

Daughters of the Sun and Moon

Lisa See · Scribner · June 9, 2026

The New York Times bestselling author's new novel builds to the night of October 24, 1871. Dr. Chee Long Tong, the herbalist at the center of Let Me Go!, appears in both works. See also serves on the board of the 1871 Memorial Project.

05 Technical overview

What the stage needs

The staging is spare by design: two playing zones, a work table for the photographer's darkroom, and an isolated chair downstage where each possession takes place. A full stage plan and tech rider are available on request.

  • InstrumentConcert grand piano with open lid access; inside-the-piano techniques are used
  • ProjectionProjector and upstage screen for archival photographs that develop and fade on cue
  • LightingRed darkroom wash, one spotlight, and a strobe effect in movement 4
  • PropsWork table, chair, developing trays, and a clothesline for hanging prints
  • SpaceRecital halls, black boxes, museum auditoriums, and chamber series venues
  • AdvisoryDepicts racial violence and lynching; includes strobe lighting. Recommended for ages 14 and up.
